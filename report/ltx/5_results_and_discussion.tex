\subsection{Experimental Campaign}

The experimental campaign was conducted in three phases: commissioning, electric motor driven pump testing, then integrated turbine and pump testing. The standalone turbine testing was not conducted due to time constraints.

\cref{fig:dim-hq} Shows the actual achieved head rise across flow rates at the different RPMs that were tested. Overlayed are solid and dashed lines. Both are theoretical head-flow curves generated from \cref{eq:lock_static_head_flow}. The dashed line is with exactly the parameters from Lock's paper, and the solid line is with the pre-rotation $K$ increased from \valLockKOneOriginal to \valLockKOneModified. The dashed vertical dashed line shows the point at a synchronized video camera detected cavitation within the diffuser through the transparent diffuser wall. It can be seen that the original parameters overpredict the head rise, and there are many mechanisms that could be causing this. 

The efficiency curve generated by measuring shaft power, subtracting mechanical losses can be shown in the following graph:

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{results_eta_flow.pdf}
    \label{fig:eff}
\end{figure}

The right graph shows the predicted efficiency curve from pure theory, and the right graph shows the curve but with adjusted disc friction. To explain the disc friction adjustment, the pump data at zero flow where all the shaft power was only going into hydraulic losses:

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{churning_power.png}
    \label{fig:churning}
\end{figure}

\cref{fig:churning} shows that the measured disc friction losses are about \valPumpChurnMult times higher than barske's formula for disc friction. Adjusting the disc friction by this factor leads to a much better fit of the efficiency curve, as can be seen in \cref{fig:eff}.

Specifically for rocket engine applications, the cavitation behaviour is of high importance. The following graph shows the cavitation curve generated by plotting the head at which cavitation was observed at different flow rates:

\begin{figure}[H]

    \label{fig:npshr}
\end{figure}

\cref{fig:npshr} shows the NPSH required to avoid cavitation at different flow rates. The dashed line is the theoretical curve generated by solving \cref{eq:lock_diffuser_throat} for the flow rate at which the head at the diffuser throat is equal to the vapor pressure head.
